Rook Dispatch · Release 4.2 review

Final report · Claude Code for PMs, Module 1 to 6

Release 4.2 made good responders
go quiet, and the fix is in a prototype.

On 12 August we shortened the ping wait from 90 to 60 seconds and changed how responders are ranked. Since then, responders miss far more pings, and some good responders stopped getting callouts in their own areas. The one change that most likely caused the rise in misses is the shorter wait. We have not tested that yet.

01 · What changed

Three changes shipped together on 12 August

Each one is visible in the config and the code. They shipped together, so the data cannot tell us which one did the damage.

Timeout

Ping wait: 90s → 60s

A ping not answered in time counts as missed and goes to the next responder.

config.py line 9: OFFER_TIMEOUT_SECONDS = 60

Ranking

Proximity up, recent acceptance down

Proximity weight .45 → .60. Recent acceptance weight .40 → .25. Capability stays at .15.

config.py lines 13 to 15

Console

Filters persist

Handler console filters now stay set between sessions. This is separate from the responder problem.

From the 4.2 release notes on the wiki.

The mechanism

A miss costs the same as a turn-down

Taking a callout adds +0.08 to a responder's score. A turn-down or a miss takes away 0.12. There is no recovery path, and scores never ease back.

offer.py:28 and offer.py:30; values in config.py:18–19

03 · What we know, and what we don't

Stated plainly

Verified

Misses went up, refusals did not

Re-run from the database for this report. Counts match the figures in the working notes.

Earlier analysis

Time between a miss and the next ping fell

Median gap: 92 seconds before, 62 seconds after. This matches the wait change. From earlier analysis; not re-run here.

Verified

The score rules are in the code

Taking a callout adds 0.08. A turn-down or miss takes away 0.12. Nothing else adds points. Read in history.py and offer.py.

Likely, not tested

The 60s wait is the most direct cause of more misses

Slow-but-willing responders may be timing out. We have no response-time logs to prove it.

Not explained

Why the four stopped getting callouts in their own areas

Their own-area offers fell from about 82% to about 21.5% (earlier analysis). Their scores were still high on 11 Aug, so falling scores alone do not explain it. Possible causes: they were dropped from the available list, travel-time estimates changed, or the live scores differ from our replay. None of these can be checked from the database or the code.

05 · The fix

Show it, don't describe it

Helen asked for something she could click through before anything in the code changes. The prototype below is that. It links the handler console and Vesper's phone, and runs on demo timing (10 seconds per ping) so the whole flow fits in a meeting.

1

See the miss

The phone shows the miss, and the handler sees the responder flagged Quiet.

2

Confirm you're here

One tap restores own-area callouts for a set time, and the phone confirms "Back in the order."

3

Get the callout, see the way

The accepted callout shows an area map and steps: On the way, Arrived, Closed.

06 · Prototype features

What I built into the prototype

Each feature below is in the file now. Where a feature depends on a decision that is not made, the card says so.

🔔

Missed-ping banner

When a ping times out, the phone says the callout moved on, with a Dismiss button. Nothing is hidden from the responder.

🌙

Quiet mode

After 3 misses in a row (placeholder threshold), the phone asks the responder to confirm they are available. The handler console flags them Quiet.

✅

Confirm sheet

"Yes, I'm available" or "Not now." One tap is all it takes. Confirming clears the miss-in-a-row count.

↩️

Back in the order

The first callout taken after confirming shows a "Back in the order" message, so the recovery is visible to the responder.

🗺️

Accepted-callout map

The responder sees the area and an estimated time. The exact address is only shown when they tap "On the way."

🚶

On the way → Arrived → Closed

Three steps that move the callout forward. The phone has an Arrived button, so the handler knows when the responder is on scene.

📍

Open in Maps and arrival notes

Shortcuts for navigation and notes for the callout. Whether callouts hold addresses or arrival notes is not checked in the database yet.

🚫

Can't make it

Treated as a turn-down that moves the callout on. Under today's scoring it costs the same as a miss. That needs Helen's review before it is shown to responders.

🔔

Test ring

The responder can ring their own phone and answer "Yes, I heard it" or "No, I didn't." A "No" is logged for support as "did not hear it." This is local only, not push delivery.

🖥️

Handler console

Live queue, responder table with Quiet and Restored pills, and a detail drawer. Demo and Real speed toggles (10 s vs 60 s), sound, annotate, reset, and Send callout.

review-checklist checks a brief before it goes further

It runs the same seven checks on any brief, so the review does not depend on how I explain it that day. It reads the brief text only. It does not query data or the wiki.

06
Out of scopeA section must exist and say what the work will not do.
