# Review checklist for a product brief

Use this checklist on the brief the user points you to. Judge the brief text only. Do not query databases or the wiki unless the user asks you to.

If the user does not name a file, ask which one. Do not guess.

## The seven checks

For each check, record PASS, PARTIAL, or FAIL, and quote the exact text you judged (or write "absent").

1. **Owner.** A named person or role who decides and answers for the work. A team name or a bare "PM" is PARTIAL. A "For:" line naming the audience is not an owner.
2. **Success measures.** At least one measure with a baseline, a target, and a timeframe. A target with no baseline is PARTIAL. A measure with no target is FAIL.
3. **Scope matches.** Compare the summary and the goals with the out-of-scope list. FAIL if something the summary or goals propose also appears as out of scope, or if the closing scope is wider or narrower than the opening scope without saying why.
4. **Problem before solution.** The problem section comes before any solution or proposal section. FAIL if a fix is proposed before the problem is described.
5. **Goals.** A section lists what the work should achieve. Goals that are only restated solutions are PARTIAL.
6. **Out of scope.** A section lists what the work will not do. FAIL if it is absent.
7. **Problem supported by data.** Each number in the problem section names its source (dataset, analysis, document, or date range). Claims with no source are FAIL. Opinions, such as "users seem unhappy," are PARTIAL unless backed by data.

## Output

1. A table with columns: Check, Result, Evidence (the quoted text or "absent").
2. A "Fix first" list of up to three items, most important first. Each item says what to change, in one sentence.
3. A one-line overall verdict: ready, ready after fixes, or not ready.

## Rules

- Do not rewrite the brief unless the user asks for a rewrite.
- Do not invent sources or data. If a number has no source in the brief, say so.
- Keep the review in plain English. Short sentences. No jargon.
